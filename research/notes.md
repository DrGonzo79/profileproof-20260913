## Useful public details

The public email proposes a job-seeker-side version of employer social screening: review the same broad red-flag categories, rank potentially concerning content, and suggest whether to remove, hide, explain, or leave it. It names nursing and frequent job changers as possible early segments, suggests a $49 one-time report, and an annual maintenance upsell. The cited market signal is that employers increasingly inspect social profiles and a vendor claims 85% have rejected a candidate based on what they found.

The linked detail page redirected to login during research, so no paywalled score, keyword, pricing, growth, execution-difficulty, revenue-potential, offer-ladder, or GTM fields were available. None are invented here. Claims are hypotheses requiring direct validation; vendor marketing is not independent evidence.

## Product interpretation

The safest useful wedge is not “beat background checks” or automatic deletion. It is a private, candidate-controlled context checklist before a specific job search. The prototype therefore uses explicit fictional fixtures and deterministic phrase matches, preserves user agency, and repeatedly states that a flag is neither misconduct nor a hiring prediction.
